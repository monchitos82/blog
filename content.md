## Rule of law (2026-11-09)

One of my favorite courses during my master's certainly was international law. One of the principles we extensively discussed during part of it was the concept of rule of law. If you search online for it, the definition is extensive and hard to grasp, but I think our professor then did a splendid job making it simple enough to fit in a sentence, by using a legal maxim: "Everything which is not forbidden, is allowed".

That sentence stuck with me and has been useful in navigating a bunch of situations and understanding the thinking of people smarter than me, who can find loopholes and apply them towards their benefit. Is this of questionable ethics? Maybe, but again, leaving loose ends in a system is the danger you create.

And that's the itch I have as I work with agents nowadays. I think many engineers are afraid of extending capabilities to their LLMs, not because they are inherently dangerous, but because they have access to a vast amount of knowledge and they are capable of exploiting our own ignorance. This gap cannot really be solved in a single step. The agents are not inventive, but they will apply what we have taught them in spaces we haven't thought of, so understanding that not forbidding things is implicitly allowing them turns a paramount part of our design. Clarity is the name of the game, and "assuming that others will assume" is a perilous gamble.

I think agentic development is forcing us towards a broader way of thinking about solving problems. It does not stop in the dilemma of “could vs. should”, it pushes us to think not only about what good design is, but what makes a good rule good: explicit, scoped, actionable, enforceable, consistent and relevant just to mention some qualities. In the end, understanding what makes actions within our own social systems controllable is key for us to design proper agentic environments where we can get the most value out of the actions we direct.

Or, we can spend a vast amount of time reading and watching courses of people who claim they discovered the formula to master their agents just to realize they are pretty much narrating the zen of Python rebranded.
