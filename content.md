## Industrialization of code (2026-11-01)

I have discussed AI topics a couple of times in this blog, but I think there is one topic that I have not covered yet and I might be following the wrong channels, because I don’t see it being discussed in many places either. AI is changing the software industry as intelligence races towards becoming a commodity. Not only this switches several paradigms, it will push ourselves to think of software less of a pair of custom-made leather shoes and start seeing it as some sneakers instead.

I have been learning and working on understanding a bunch of principles and I see there is a lot of focus towards picking what is the right way to plan for asking AI to produce code, the more now that code can be built with a certain degree of confidence. What I don’t see everywhere is the same amount of focus towards using what the transformation into a mass-production level of industry requires. I think we are still too obsessed with laying out our tools that we don’t fully see the need for other parts of this “new” machine: measuring the outputs, measuring the processes, improving the supply chain, just to name a few. And this, personally, is quite an exciting shift, because we are speeding towards the transformation from being the artisans known as software engineers, to the industrial engineers, creating product lines and distribution channels.

I don’t have much to propose in this brief thesis. My idea takes a few analogies like the footwear one. I think we will sooner or later introduce what industrial engineering has been using for decades: quality control, workflow improvement, supply chain optimization. In all fairness, the software industry _as-is_, is not agnostic to these techniques and processes. However, the scale is changing fast and we are not only choosing between the corner shop and the multi-acre assembly line, as every individual become their own software factory, we are now forcing ourselves not only to figure out our production standards, but we are now forcing ourselves to focus on measuring the output rather than the product and between creating an experience like car dealerships or the customized Jordan sneakers checkout page, I hope we opt for the latter.

 Anyway, I am excited for this change and the opportunity it will create. We will see a new level of focus in all parts of a production process, from picking the right tools and setting them up in the right order as once Toyota engineers taught us. Improving the production lines continuously, not only the product we build. Improving the supply chains and logistics associated. Changing things like quality assurance to not only do a coverage analysis of unit cases but actually measure the output of our product lines and the quality of what is produced by our machines (agents). We will take observability to a different level. Measuring resource utilization, throughput, acceptance, rework and why not, even repurposing.

Is this enthusiasm too sudden and too naive? I don’t think so. I think many parts of this industry (the software industry) will remain, just as you get to commission a knife to a Japanese blacksmith or a pair of shoes to an Italian artisan. Demand will remain for certain classes of products, and it will require a high degree of specialized skills to still exist and improve. For the rest, I hope we improve the parts where supply is still very rough (e.g. package managers and app stores) and accept that quality is good enough for the bulk of simple and repeatable operations.
