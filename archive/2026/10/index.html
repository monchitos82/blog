Literacy is not optional (2026-10-26)

I like using LLMs as much as I liked AI technologies before GAI was simply referred to as AI. One of the things I used to like about it was the degree of specialization it performed tasks at a far deeper level compared to a human or even a deterministic process. As generative AI gained traction, one of the things I liked the least from it was the narrative of it being a good replacement for experts and knowledge. As a tool, it is a great tool, but tools require skill to be used properly. I despise how tech executives dismissed the impact these technologies had on education and how important it has become for us as a society to protect our younger generations from delegating their thoughts to a probabilistic tool.

Not long ago, I heard from a friend and a very respected acquaintance that their kids started their education in Computer Science. Some would claim that is a terrible move considering the speed at which AI is replacing roles and activities in that specific field and while it is frightening the uncertainty this creates, I think we are being dismissive of a larger problem that is surging: We are running short of experts fast. Literacy is a non-negotiable quality for any profession and acting as it is optional is a naive and very dangerous gamble.

I have no plan on glorifying myself or my experience, I sometimes delegate learning things lazily to an AI model, but I reckon often that is a mistake. I don't think I am the only one thinking that way. When speed to market is everything, we find it easy to just assume the agents will take care and do a good job, and while they have improved to an extent we can trust what they produce is going to work, we are still not free of the responsibility of knowing what we are doing and the consequences of doing it wrong. Kids educating themselves in this field, and actually in any, are doing the right thing, because even if we insist that the models will do just as well if not better, the models are incapable of imagining adverse scenarios, let alone understanding consequences. We need people to learn and grow experts into what they do so they can actually produce good software, even if they are not the ones writing it. I will close this thought with an anecdote:

Today I finally got my fingerprint reader working in Linux. The drivers produced by the manufacturer (Goodix) always worked in Windows, but it has been a couple years since I left Windows entirely and this was the only "sacrifice" I made. I knew there was a working port for this driver somewhere in AUR, but this was tied to an old version of the fprintd library. Well, today I put agy to work and got that patch merged into a newer version of the library, so in the end I had a working version of the library and the fingerprint reader actually being recognized. Had I been more naive, I would have bragged about this and even dare to open a pull-request to the library, except that 1) I am reusing an unofficial patch that someone more skilled than me already wrote. 2) I have no effin idea of how to write a driver, I just directed a merge and compiled it. 3) I will, from now on, rely on a drifting version of a library so patches and updates I have to apply myself for this to keep working. I can live with my own can of worms because this is my own computer and I know the risks I am taking when using this approach, so not only did I have to check for uncovered CVEs but actually restrain myself from installing this for things like sudo or passkeys. But I have been around. I have made my fair share of mistakes and more importantly, age and experience have made me aware and humble, LLMs are lacking those two qualities and we will keep needing people being able to call that out.

So I think LLMs, AI, or whatever you want to call these tools are great for this line of work, but they should never, ever be used to replace learning, curiosity and eventually judgement, on the contrary, we will run out of people with these qualities and for now I am glad that young people are being brave enough to try regardless of what some doomer with a big mouth and an even bigger pocket says.
