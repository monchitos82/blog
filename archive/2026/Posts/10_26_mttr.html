MTTR (10/26)

This is not a post discussing what MTTR - Mean Time to Repair means, it is my case against using it to measure your team's capacity to act against an incident, especially in complex systems.

Unlike other metrics like time to detect, the time to repair tends to lean towards the subjective end in its definition and creates a strong dependency towards 3 criteria: 1. Domain over a system, 2. Simplicity of the system and 3. Control over dependencies. To make matters worse, the time to resolution has a direct impact over an error budget, however, it is not indicative of an engineers’ team efficiency, this becomes evident when incidents can be associated with data or the result of a cascading failure.

On large systems, with a vast amount of dependencies and a massive scale, it is paramount to understand if a time to repair means time to restore the operability of a system or is it considering the time to to restore its capacity, it is not the same, and when data and scale are combined, the difference is not small. As we know (or assume), data is easier to produce than to consume. This complexity grows as transformation and processing are brought into our systems. The impact this single statement has over time to resolve incidents when we consider restoring our processing and ingestion pipelines can become of magnitude of days and lowering this metric not only impacts directly our engineering time, it will likely impact our budget.

Just like managers solely focusing on SLOs, I have dealt with managers solely focusing on mean time metrics, the result is likely similar, we measure something, but that something is not our team’s efficiency. I think that having a clear distinction (domain) between when a system’s rollback action means returning to stability and a partial recovery is important to make a judgement call on what metric is the right metric to define both our system stability and our team’s capacity to handle an incident. Going a step further, using these metrics with clarity is not a bad call when we leverage on these to measure the feasibility of both our level objectives and our error budget, so in a sense, repair mean times are better suited to give us insight on gauging engineering capacity to align with our service levels.

So I am not entirely against using MTTR per-se, I am against using it as a metric for incident management efficiency, especially, when data is the name of the game.
