Operational excellence (04/26)

As we delve into site reliability, I like to make a distinction; I see site reliability as a mindset rather than just a position. A mindset that values business continuity. It is also a mindset that acknowledges that operating with excellence includes being ready for an inevitable failure and implementing an effective strategy of containment and mitigation.

I have seen many companies jump straight into creating a team just to get lost in a sea of bureaucracy, signal noise and artifacts that ironically become toil or by centralizing the effort just to come to the conclusion that the centralization created a bottleneck. I also have made the effort to unwind this mess by transferring back the ability to run their systems to the engineers themselves. It is not easy and definitely not immediate.

If there is a justification for a team, it is for them to serve as advisors, using their expertise to define good practices that help our systems attain these four qualities: measurability, predictability, repeatability and control. We can also use that expertise to implement a feedback loop that can benefit our organization and its ability to operate to the highest standards of resiliency. For the rest of our organization, it just makes sense to prioritize the ability to operate our business over any other initiative. Sometimes we may choose to run in a degraded state or accept consuming our error budget, however, these decisions should never be permanent. Ultimately stability is paramount for the benefit of our business.
